# Project-specific pricing decisions

Read the main pricing/source guide for book rates. Use these additional rules when billing units or offered equipment differ from the solicitation.

## Standing service basis and frequency

The user approved this standing rule on **November 15, 2026**: standalone service prices are **per visit**; recommend **two visits per month** when the contract does not state frequency. The [rental pricing guide](rental-pricing-guide.md) and [catalog](../assets/pricing/rate-catalog.json) record that authority separately from PDF rate provenance.

Apply an explicit contract frequency or current project-approved plan before this recommendation. Two visits/month means twice monthly; it is not every two weeks. For a 14-day, weekly, daily, per-activation or on-demand requirement, use the actual cycle and performance dates. Do not replace it with two visits/month.

Extend a known visit amount by the actual service-unit count and visit count. A $600 visit at the recommended two visits/month is $1,200/month per applicable service unit; no new approval is needed for that standing calculation. A monthly pricing form gets the extended monthly amount and stated visit basis, not an unconverted per-visit price. An unpriced service amount, unsupported cycle conversion or additional commitment still needs its own answer. Once a fixed monthly service price is approved for a required cycle, do not impose an invented visit cap or surcharge.

This default does not relabel equipment-with-service offerings as standalone service amounts, invent cleaning/restocking dollar rates, or override explicit staffing/day, consumables/week and accessory/month prices. Preserve service inclusions and avoid counting an included pump-out twice. Existing project-specific approved prices remain scoped to their projects.

## Buyer pricing forms and deviations

Use exact mandatory rows, units, periods and order. Include all costs required by the buyer; do not add transport/setup surcharges outside an all-inclusive schedule. Known logistics components can remain internal costing inputs until their treatment is approved. Do not amortize them over an assumed award date or guaranteed rental term.

Follow buyer rules on alternatives, qualifications and exceptions. If company inventory differs, follow [product and package selection](product-selection.md): compare suitable listed options with proposed values, recommend one and obtain the user's selection unless already supplied or explicitly delegated. Then draft the actual chosen offering, flag the mismatch and identify any required written clarification. Do not change the buyer's stated requirement or claim acceptance because the offered unit is larger. A unit's image or user-approved rental amount does not establish electrical rating, tank capacity, fixtures, new/unused condition or availability.

## Historical worked decision — not a future default

For Texas DPS `405-27R0001325`, the user specifically approved:

- A three-stall offering because two-stall inventory was unavailable, while the SOW specified two stalls. Written DPS clarification remained outstanding.
- $3,995 per month for rental.
- $600 pumping plus $700 cleaning = $1,300 **per service visit**.
- A fixed monthly cleaning/pumping rate of **$2,816.67**, after being shown `$1,300 × 26 ÷ 12` and the possibility of a 27-visit calendar.
- The same rates for all four renewal years; combined monthly rate **$6,811.67**.

These are project-specific approvals. Do not reuse them as universal rates, assume a three-stall restroom-only model was verified, impose a 26-visit cap, or claim transport/tank package coverage was confirmed. Final model, 110V/20A compatibility, 350-gallon wastewater minimum and additional potable-water package remained checks for that project.
