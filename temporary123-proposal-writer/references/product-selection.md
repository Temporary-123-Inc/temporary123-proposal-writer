# Product selection and package comparison

Use this process when requested equipment has no exact supported product match, or several required products/units need a meaningful package choice. An exact, supported current-project selection can proceed without a redundant choice question. Use choices the human proposal writer/user has already made for this project; historical choices are not fleet or pricing defaults.

If one exact supported product meets the whole scope and no alternative comparison or unresolved equipment choice is requested, use the normal matching process without forcing a package comparison. If the scope actually needs multiple units/types, compare useful packages unless the user has already chosen or delegated the mix; an exact model within a package does not remove that package decision.

## Read requirements and real product evidence

Read the current solicitation's functional requirements and alternatives policy, the [company/source guide](sources-and-company.md), applicable descriptions/qualifications in the [price-book PDF](../assets/rental-price-book-reading-copy.pdf) and [searchable text](../assets/rental-price-book.txt), and matching rows in the [actual trailer catalog](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1tu6szuEWANuI0gtqrsl9g_gQa0Vm256YD7UmsEtouOc/edit?gid=943101906#gid=943101906). Open relevant linked equipment folders using the [catalog-reading instructions](trailer-images.md); empty search results require direct folder browsing. Read [pricing rules](pricing-rules.md) before valuing a candidate.

Record the actual source/file/page/row, extracted configuration and rate basis for each candidate. Use product names/model labels that the source supports. Do not invent SKUs, turn a broad folder category into an exact model, infer specifications from price or imagery, or imply a catalog entry is in stock. Record required available-unit counts as confirmed or open checks using actual evidence.

Compare quantity and per-unit versus aggregate requirements; fixtures/functions, accessibility, tank capacities, power/water/waste, dimensions/footprint/access, delivery/setup/removal and service needs; performance dates and source-supported unit count; and whether alternatives or combinations are permitted. A larger unit is not automatically compliant, equivalent or buyer-approved. If alternatives are prohibited, do not present a substitution as an eligible base offer. Clearly distinguish a technical candidate needing written buyer clarification from an accepted option.

## Build defensible options and coverage

Consider a single substitute, repeated units of the same actual product and mixed-product packages when each is supported and useful for the actual requirement. Suggest combinations when several items/units are needed, without adding equipment, occupancy demand or service frequency the scope does not establish.

For each option, state its actual product mix/quantities, supported coverage, surplus, unmet requirements and unknown checks. Add capacities only when their function, units, timing and operating assumptions are compatible and the buyer allows aggregate coverage. Multiple smaller units do not satisfy a per-unit requirement merely because their totals add up. Do not sum toilets with showers, maximum occupancy with fixture count, incompatible throughput measures, or separate tanks into an unsupported shared capacity. Check accessibility and utilities for the actual arrangement rather than assuming that one component solves every unit's requirement.

Show two or three useful options when the sources actually support that choice. Show fewer if only one defensible candidate exists; show none when no defensible fit is established and ask for the specific missing product evidence or direction. Do not manufacture options to fill a table. Label one best-supported candidate **Recommended — provisional** and give a brief reason based on functional fit, practical deployment/service and supported value. Keep its unresolved checks and buyer-acceptance needs visible.

## Proposed value and billing basis

For each candidate, calculate sourced quantity × rate × supported billable-period count using `Decimal`, preserving the actual rate unit and inclusion rules. For service/visit items use their supported quantity and billing basis rather than automatically multiplying by trailer count. Read the relevant [pricing rules](pricing-rules.md) for conversions, transport/setup treatment, scope matching and source authority.

Separate rental, servicing and logistics amounts. Sum only compatible billing periods/bases and avoid double-counting included services. Show recurring monthly/weekly/event values and one-time transport/setup/removal separately unless a complete, supported common-term calculation is possible. Do not invent a rental term, number of visits, daily proration, bundle discount, missing rate or historical-project price to produce a package total.

If an amount is unsupported, show the **supported subtotal** with its basis plus the specific unknown components; do not call it a complete total or all-inclusive price. This comparison can inform selection while required quote checks are outstanding, but final required commercial inputs still need answers or explicit incomplete-draft direction.

## Present options and obtain the choice early

Use a compact table in chat/working notes:

| Option (Recommended label) | Product mix & quantities | Fit/differences | Proposed value & billing basis | Source/open checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Link the source products/pages/rows and show the reason for the provisional recommendation. Keep the table outside the buyer-facing Word proposal unless the buyer requires formal alternates or the user approves their inclusion. When formal alternates are included, follow the buyer's required format and disclose differences accurately.

Ask the human proposal writer/user to select an option early, with the provisional recommendation and only the unresolved questions needed for the current scope. Do not wait for the final resource-table review or ask again when the current-project selection is already clear. If the user has explicitly delegated the selection, make the reasoned choice and explain it while preserving unresolved requirements. Continue independent research, company sections and other supported drafting while waiting. Silence is not selection: do not finalize an unresolved equipment plan without the user's choice, explicit delegation or explicit instruction to deliver an incomplete draft.

Record the selected product mix and quantities as a project-specific decision and update the [workflow source-status table](proposal-workflow.md#9-review-resource-table--resolve-questions). A user's choice authorizes drafting that actual offering; it does not establish inventory availability, cure unmet requirements or grant buyer acceptance. Keep required written clarification and other open technical/commercial checks visible, and apply the final required-input gate before delivery.
