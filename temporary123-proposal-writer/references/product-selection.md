# Product alternatives and package selection

Use this branch during [workflow step 3](proposal-workflow.md) when a requested product has no exact supported catalog match, or the scope needs multiple units or different product types. Price the options using step 5. Keep using the existing product and pricing libraries; this reference adds a selection method, not a second catalog or new default rates. An exact supported single-product match does not need an extra selection question.

If one exact supported product meets the whole scope and no alternative comparison or unresolved equipment choice is requested, use the normal matching process without forcing a package comparison. If the scope actually needs multiple units/types, compare useful packages unless the user has already chosen or delegated the mix; an exact model within a package does not remove that package decision.

## 1. Establish the need and catalog evidence

Read the current scope and separate explicit requirements from preferences: function, quantity/capacity, configuration, accessibility, utilities, footprint/access, dates, service and any limits on alternatives. Do not change an exact requirement into a minimum without evidence. Ask for an ambiguity only when it affects the choice.

Read the [inventory specification guide](inventory-specifications.md) and query the [technical inventory index](../assets/inventory/product-specifications.json) first for actual product/asset facts; inspect the cited cells in the [original workbook](../assets/inventory/temporary123-inventory-and-specifications.xlsx) when needed. Use ItemID rather than repeated SKU as the key and review missing/conflicting fields. Then read relevant [price-book pages](../assets/rental-price-book-reading-copy.pdf), [matching and pricing rules](pricing-and-company-sources.md), [equipment image catalog](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1tu6szuEWANuI0gtqrsl9g_gQa0Vm256YD7UmsEtouOc/edit?gid=943101906#gid=943101906), matching linked product/specification folders and any current user-approved updates or quote. Use the [supporting repository](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1tVYaXws6YOkV-k2fik05WEv8xXxl22Q7) for further evidence. Check relevant folders directly when search is empty. Do not use a nearby priced size or generic photo category to fill a missing asset specification or rate.

Record actual listed product names/configurations and source pages/rows. A price-book listing or image does not prove stock, available quantity, exact fixtures or availability on the requested dates. Keep a shower/restroom combination distinct from restroom-only equipment; keep accessibility and other material features explicit.

## 2. Build suitable options or combinations

Show a small, useful set of supported alternatives, normally two or three when there are that many suitable choices. Show one if only one defensible candidate exists; never invent products or pad the list with unsuitable options. If no suitable catalog option can be evidenced, explain the uncovered need and ask for an additional approved product/source or direction.

Consider a single substitute, repeated units of a listed model, or a mix of products when they address the actual need. For each package, show product names, quantities and which requested functions/capacities each supplies. State differences, excess capacity, unmet requirements and additional site/service needs. Do not add accessories or services that the scope does not need merely to make a bundle.

For a shared compatible capacity, calculate the required unit count from the evidenced effective capacity and round up when appropriate. Confirm the relevant layout, allocation, access and operating constraints rather than assuming all capacity can be pooled. A per-unit requirement must be met by each applicable unit; aggregate tank volumes, fixtures or other totals do not cure a deficient individual unit. Do not add unlike functions, incompatible capacities or price-book planning allowances to claim guaranteed occupancy or compliance. More stalls alone do not establish accessibility, power/tank compatibility or buyer acceptance.

A request for two stalls with only a three-stall candidate is a mismatch to disclose and compare. It does not justify inventing a two-stall product, automatically replacing it, or applying a combination-trailer rate to restroom-only equipment. Use an accurately labeled conditional candidate where technical evidence or acceptance remains unresolved. If the solicitation prohibits the alternative, explain that obstacle; user selection cannot establish buyer acceptance.

## 3. Calculate each option's proposed value

Use a current user-approved quote for this project or matching published book rates. Read [pricing decisions and conversions](pricing-decisions.md). Use decimal arithmetic for quantity × rate × supported periods or service visits, with deliberate currency rounding.

Keep the components visible: equipment rental by item, applicable recurring service/support, one-time mobilization/demobilization and transport, then other required costs. Service and visit charges follow their sourced quantity and billing basis, not automatic multiplication by trailer count. Apply costs per unit or package exactly as sourced; do not assume one trailer's logistics/service charge covers an entire fleet, or count an included component twice. Do not invent a bundle discount, proration, renewal rate, tax policy or accessory rate.

Compare options on the same duration, scope, currency and billing basis. Sum compatible recurring charges for the same period; show one-time charges separately. Show a full-term total only when duration, charging rules and required components are supported. A monthly figure and a weekly figure cannot be added as one recurring rate without an approved valid conversion.

When a required component is unknown, show the sourced **rental subtotal** or other clearly labeled **partial estimate**, identify each missing component and ask for it. Do not present that subtotal as an all-inclusive package price. If the product itself has no supported rate, say that its price is needed; retain any independently priced components. Prior project approvals remain specific to their original project.

## 4. Recommend and ask for the selection

Present this compact table in chat, adding a separate cost breakdown if needed:

| Option | Product mix and quantities | Fit and differences | Proposed value and billing basis | Sources / open checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Label one option **Recommended — provisional** while awaiting selection or material checks, when there is a defensible preference. Explain the reason in plain language: supported coverage of the need, site fit, service/logistics burden and evidenced cost. Do not rank solely by stall count or the cheapest rental if other material costs or requirements differ. Mark the recommendation provisional when important evidence is pending; distinguish the lowest known rental from the lowest complete cost.

Ask the human proposal writer/user which option or combination to use and what required missing facts/prices they can supply. Use the available question tool when appropriate and include the visible option names and proposed values; a preselected recommendation or silence is not a submitted selection. Do not re-ask a choice already made for this project unless a material change creates a new decision. A user who has explicitly delegated the selection may be given the reasoned choice directly, with unresolved requirements still disclosed.

Continue independent company/reference research and supported drafting while the choice is pending. Finalize dependent equipment and pricing only after the selection and required answers, or explicit instruction to deliver an incomplete draft. User approval of an alternative is separate from any required buyer clarification or acceptance; do not contact the buyer without authorization.

Record the chosen package, quantities, rates/sources, approval and remaining conditions in working notes and the source-status table. Reconcile the choice throughout scope, specifications, service, images, pricing and required forms. Keep the comparison and recommendation outside the buyer-facing Word proposal unless the buyer requests formal alternatives or the user asks to include them. Preserve required buyer line items and do not silently change the solicitation's quantities to fit the recommendation.
