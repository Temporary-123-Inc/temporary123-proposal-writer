# Proposal blueprint

This writing guide distills the previously reviewed Temporary 123 proposal for temporary living quarters, showers, and heads: https://docs.google.com/document/d/1rG5FWpSKjbR6IkqA11jhGdmRs_BpE-Ac4OQ7bB4EYzQ/edit . The complete inspected snapshot is `assets/reference-export.pdf`. Use the guide to write a fresh draft from the user's new project link. The old Google Doc does not need to be opened or copied. Follow the new solicitation's required structure when it differs.

Apply the source-use and missing-information rules in `SKILL.md` throughout this blueprint. Use existing company, pricing and past-performance records before asking questions. Resolve required missing facts with the user before delivery; placeholders are permitted in a delivered document only when the user explicitly requests an incomplete draft. Keep completion instructions outside the buyer-facing proposal. Normal unsigned execution lines remain appropriate.

## Front matter

### Cover page

For the default Word proposal, use a clean title/details page or opening block with the response type, preparation date or supplied response date, solicitation number, project title, prepared-for agency/customer and contact, and prepared-by company/contact. Add verified registration identifiers when relevant. Leave execution dates unsigned where required. Keep content editable; do not reuse old project text embedded in cover artwork.

### Cover letter

Include a dated business cover letter in every default Word proposal, before the contents and body. Follow the previously reviewed letter's structure and voice, or a newer user-designated cover letter. Use a recipient block and matching salutation. Open with the proposer, solicitation, requested services, and place of performance. Summarize supported qualifications and the proposed scope, then highlight pricing approach, relevant experience, applicable documentation, and schedule readiness. Close with an invitation to clarify and an unsigned name/title/contact block. Keep the project summary consistent with the technical and price sections. Aim for about one page rather than repeating the entire proposal.

### Table of contents

Use Word heading styles and actual final page references. Include body sections and meaningful subsections. Use an automatic/native contents mechanism where available and verify its displayed entries after rendering; do not retain sample page numbers. For an explicitly requested chat draft, omit pagination.

## 1. Company Overview

Explain the business, relevant service lines, geographical coverage, supported customers, and experience as evidenced by the supplied company information. Connect those capabilities to this buyer's need. Include only verified registrations, contract vehicles, certifications, deployment capabilities, and service commitments.

**Core Company Information** follows as labeled fields: legal business name; address; website; SAM status, UEI and CAGE when relevant; primary and other relevant NAICS codes; proposal contact. Use sourced information and ask about required unverified fields. Omit optional unsupported claims. An old proposal alone is insufficient to assert that a registration is currently active.

## 2. Technical Approach

Open with a project-specific turnkey solution: what is supplied, who it supports, and which lifecycle services are included. Tie the offer to the supplied SOW's quantities, functional requirements, and operating conditions. Describe responsibilities and handoff points so the buyer can understand how delivery will work.

### Phase I: Pre-Deployment Coordination

Address the project schedule and milestones, site/access coordination, placement plan, utility verification, permits or clearances if required, buyer/contractor responsibilities and readiness dependencies. Include supported dates and ask about required missing commitments; do not promise immediate deployment simply because the source does.

### Phase II: Delivery and Installation

Describe transportation and staging; delivery location and access windows; placement, leveling, and safe setup; electrical/water/waste connections; testing; inspection and acceptance. Use the offered equipment's real utility requirements rather than automatically repeating the sample's 240V/50A values. State who supplies utilities and who makes connections.

### Phase III: Operations and Maintenance

Use these practical subtopics where applicable:

- **Routine Servicing:** supported frequency, potable-water resupply, wastewater removal, cleaning, consumables, and preventive maintenance.
- **Monitoring & Quality Control:** supported coordinator role, inspection/documentation method, reporting cadence and issue handling; ask for the assigned name only when needed for the response.
- **Emergency Support:** actual contact arrangements, service hours, escalation and supported response commitments. Ask about required unsupported service commitments.

### Phase IV: Demobilization

Explain safe utility disconnection, removal, site restoration, disposal records when required, and final inspection/closeout. State the end-of-term trigger or date.

### Regulatory Compliance and Safety

Address requirements actually relevant to the solicitation, site and supplied equipment. Describe the planned safety, waste-handling, environmental, and access arrangements using supported facts. Do not copy blanket declarations of OSHA/EPA/RCRA/DoD compliance or universal citizenship/ID requirements from the example. Use the current solicitation for clause references and flag interpretations needing confirmation.

### Utility & Infrastructure Considerations

Specify power/load and connection, potable water, wastewater containment/disposal, HVAC, site footprint and access requirements where supported. Show who provides each resource. Ask for required unsupported values rather than inventing specifications.

### Operational Readiness and Rapid Deployment

Explain equipment availability, delivery lead time, regional support and dependencies using approved information. Distinguish a required arrival date from a confirmed ability to meet it.

## 3. Equipment Specifications

Create a subsection for each offered equipment type. The example uses **Sleeper/Quarters/Berthing Trailers** and **Restroom with Shower Trailers**; replace or expand them for the new scope.

Cover quantity and model/configuration, capacity, dimensions/footprint, layout/privacy, fixtures, HVAC, power/water/waste requirements, access, safety and accessibility features, and included accessories, as relevant and supported. Connect features to the required use. Make offered occupancy distinct from a model's maximum capacity. When useful, select matching images from the user-designated catalog using [trailer-images.md](trailer-images.md); use photos/spec sheets only for the offered or clearly labeled representative configuration. Do not assume HUD certification, stackability, private rooms, Rockwool insulation, touchless fixtures or accessible configurations from the sample.

## 4. Price Proposal

Introduce the price basis, period covered and defined scope. Use the buyer's required pricing schedule/CLINs when supplied. Otherwise use a table with these columns:

| Line item | Description | Quantity | Unit/billing basis | Unit price | Extended price |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Read [pricing-and-company-sources.md](pricing-and-company-sources.md) and match rates to the retained user-supplied price book before filling the table. Candidate rows are delivery, pickup, each equipment rental, routine servicing, mobilization/setup and demobilization. Include rows only when applicable; explain what setup covers when transport is charged separately. Add total and supported quote terms. Reconcile quantities, rental term and number of service visits with the technical approach. Populate supported prices, flag unsupported components, and retain a source-page calculation record outside the proposal. The old $51,990 total is not a default quote.

## 5. Past Performance

Select relevant examples from the existing company and past-performance records, preferably three when available and no different count is required. Use their supported descriptions and contacts. Ask for additional details or examples only when the available records do not meet this response's needs; do not ask the user to recreate the library or invent a record.

Each record contains a descriptive project heading, customer/agency, contract role/type, location, performance dates, value when permitted, reference POC name/title/email/phone when approved, and a short scope/results narrative explaining relevance. Keep record fields consistent across references. The source's three headings are U.S. Navy Contract Support; Temporary Restroom and Housing Facility Rental; and Complete Turn-key Service. These are historical examples, not preapproved current references.

## 6. Required Documentation

Follow the actual solicitation's representations, certifications, acknowledgements and attachments. The source includes FAR 52.212-3 and acknowledgements for HSAR 3052.209-70 and 3052.237-72. These are conditional examples, not a universal compliance package. Do not add them solely because they occur in the old proposal.

For each applicable item, draft an appropriately qualified statement, required company identifiers and an unsigned authorized-representative block. Leave execution date and signature blank. Determine applicable requirements from the actual solicitation; ask if required instructions or attachments remain unavailable. Track missing attachments in the internal completion list and do not claim they are enclosed.

## Known source inconsistencies

Correct these patterns during adaptation rather than reproducing them:

- The body and contents skip section 3; use consecutive numbering unless the buyer prescribes otherwise.
- The recipient block and salutation disagree; use the actual recipient consistently.
- The cover-letter signature repeats a name and contains a double-dot email typo; use verified contact details.
- Primary NAICS differs between the company profile and certification; reconcile with approved current records.
- Technical occupancy and equipment maximum capacity differ; identify the proposed configuration explicitly.
- The cover contains dated project details as image text; ordinary text replacement will not update those details.
